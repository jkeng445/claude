# Playbook: Business CEO

Founders, executives, consultants, owner-operators.

**The result that matters: inbound opportunities.** Track them: sales calls, partnership asks, investor intros, strong job applicants, podcast and press invites. A CEO with 2,000 of the *right* followers can outperform one with 50,000 random ones.

---

## 1. Platform choice

| CEO type | Main | Second |
|---|---|---|
| **B2B / services / consulting** | LinkedIn | Newsletter (LinkedIn newsletter or Substack) |
| **Tech / startup / investor-facing** | LinkedIn + X | Podcast guesting |
| **Consumer brand founder** | LinkedIn (for the business) + Instagram (for the founder story) | TikTok (founder-led content) |

LinkedIn is the default. Decision-makers are there, and personal profiles get far more reach than company pages.

## 2. Positioning formula

> I help **[who]** **[achieve what]** through **[how / point of view]**, and I share **[what kind of insight]** from building **[company]**.

The best CEO accounts have a **point of view**, not just a job title. Find the belief they hold that some peers would disagree with. That belief is the content engine.

## 3. Profile setup (LinkedIn)

| Element | Do this |
|---|---|
| **Photo** | Professional, warm, recent. Face fills ~60% of the frame. |
| **Banner** | Company + what you do + one proof point, or a speaking or event photo |
| **Headline** | `[Role] @ [Company] · Helping [who] [outcome] · [proof]`, e.g. *CEO @ Acme · Helping clinics cut admin time in half · 300+ clinics* |
| **About** | 3 short paragraphs: what you believe → what you've built and the proof → what to contact you about. End with a clear contact line. |
| **Featured** | Company site, best post, a talk or interview, a booking link |
| **Follow button** | Where offered, make "Follow" (not "Connect") the main profile button so strangers can follow without a request |
| **Experience** | Each role gets 2–3 lines of *outcomes*, not duties |

## 4. Content pillars

| Pillar | Share | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Lessons from building** | 35% | "We lost our biggest client. Here's what changed." Hiring, pricing, and decisions, with real numbers where you can share them |
| **Point of view on the industry** | 30% | Contrarian takes, predictions, reactions to news |
| **Behind the company** | 20% | Team wins, product launches, customer stories (with permission) |
| **The human** | 15% | Why you started, mistakes, routines. Vulnerable, not oversharing. |

## 5. Rhythm (built for a busy executive)

- **Posts:** 3×/week (e.g. Tue / Wed / Thu mornings in the audience's time zone).
- **Comments:** 15 min/day of thoughtful comments on posts from peers, customers, and bigger voices in the industry. In the first 60 days, this drives more growth than posting.
- **Signature piece:** 1×/month, either a long post, newsletter, or short video.

**The executive workflow (about 1 hr/week of their time):**
1. A 30-min weekly voice note or interview, recorded with a ghostwriter or assistant.
2. The ghostwriter turns it into 3 posts in the CEO's voice.
3. The CEO approves and edits in 15 min, adding one personal detail per post.
4. The CEO does their own comments. Never outsource the comments. That's where the relationships come from.

## 6. Post formats that work on LinkedIn

- **Hook line → story → lesson → question.** The first two lines decide everything, because they appear before "…see more."
- **Short, scannable lines.** Write for a phone screen.
- **Document/carousel posts** for frameworks.
- **Text-only** for stories and opinions.
- **Links go in the first comment**, not the post body.
- **No engagement-bait** ("Comment YES"). It hurts credibility with exactly the people who matter.

## 7. Growth moves that actually work for CEOs

1. **Comment strategy:** pick 20 people the CEO wants to be known by (customers, peers, investors, journalists) and leave thoughtful comments on their posts every week.
2. **Employee amplification:** the team reshares with their own one-line take (optional, never forced).
3. **Podcast guesting:** 1–2 a month on niche shows. Turn each appearance into 3–5 clip posts.
4. **Co-create:** do a joint post or live session with a respected peer.
5. **Speak:** local events and industry panels, then post about them.

## 8. Realistic milestones

| Day | LinkedIn followers (new) | Avg impressions/post | Inbound opportunities |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | +200–500 | 500–2,000 | 0–2 |
| 60 | +500–1,500 | 1,000–5,000 | 2–6 |
| 90 | +1,000–3,000 | 2,000–10,000 | 5–15 (log every one) |

Inbound is the number to report to the CEO. Followers are just a leading indicator.

## 9. Mistakes to avoid
- Posting company press releases as personal posts. Nobody follows a brochure.
- A ghostwriter voice that doesn't sound like them. People who meet them notice.
- Talking about confidential numbers, clients, or employees without clearing it. One bad post costs more than 100 good ones earn.
- Starting, going silent for a month, then restarting. Pick a rhythm they can keep through a busy quarter.
