# How to actually implement this

The playbooks say *what* to do. This says *when*, in what order, and what to do
on a Tuesday when you don't feel like it.

Two phases: a **setup sprint** (about 6 hours, spread over two weeks) and then a
**weekly rhythm** (3–5 hours) that you repeat until it's a habit.

---

## Phase 1 — Setup sprint (weeks 1–2, ~6 hours total)

Do these in order. Each one depends on the one before it, and skipping ahead to
posting is the single most common way this fails.

| # | Task | Time | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fill `templates/intake-questionnaire.md` on yourself | 45 min | Every **bold** question has an answer |
| 2 | Fill `templates/positioning-worksheet.md` | 60 min | The one-liner passes the test: a competitor can't say it too |
| 3 | Claim handles everywhere; turn on 2FA | 30 min | Same handle on LinkedIn, X, Instagram, TikTok, Substack |
| 4 | Photo + banner | 60 min | A recent, well-lit head-and-shoulders photo. Phone is fine, window light, plain wall |
| 5 | Write the profile from the worksheet | 60 min | Headline, About, Featured, contact line all live |
| 6 | Write 9 posts **before announcing anything** | 120 min | 9 drafts in a doc, 3 scheduled |
| 7 | Pick your 20 target accounts | 30 min | A list of 20 people you want to be known by |

**Hard rule: don't announce until step 6 is done.** A new visitor who finds an
empty profile does not come back. Nine posts is what makes the profile look
alive.

### The photo is not optional, and it's where people stall
You don't need a photographer. Stand facing a window, not with your back to it.
Plain wall behind you. Phone on a shelf at eye height, portrait mode, self-timer.
Take 40, pick 1. This is 20 minutes, and a bad photo costs you more reach than
any post will earn.

---

## Phase 2 — The weekly rhythm (3–5 hours/week)

This is the whole job. Put it in your calendar as recurring blocks or it won't
happen.

| Day | Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Batch write | 60 min | Write the week's 3 posts in one sitting. Pull from your pillars. Don't edit yet. |
| **Mon** | — | 10 min | Edit yesterday's drafts, schedule Tue/Wed/Thu |
| **Tue** | Post + engage | 20 min | Post fires. Reply to every comment within the hour. Comment on 5 target accounts. |
| **Wed** | Post + engage | 20 min | Same |
| **Thu** | Post + engage | 20 min | Same |
| **Fri** | Outreach | 30 min | One thing that compounds: pitch a podcast, message a peer, invite a collaborator |
| **Every 2 wks** | Review | 20 min | Fill `templates/scorecard.md`. Decide one change. |

**Total: ~3 hrs/week**, with the Friday block being the first thing to drop in a
bad week.

### The engagement block matters more than the posts
In the first 60 days, commenting on other people's posts will grow you faster
than your own posts will. Your posts reach your followers; you have none yet.
Comments on a big account's post reach *their* followers.

A good comment adds a specific example, a disagreement, or a number. "Great
post!" is worse than nothing — it signals you're farming.

---

## What "done" looks like each week

Check these three, not your follower count:

- [ ] Did I publish what I planned? (If no three weeks running, cut to 2 posts/week. A plan you keep beats a plan you admire.)
- [ ] Did I comment on 15+ posts from my target list?
- [ ] Did one stranger engage with me? (comment, DM, follow-and-reply)

---

## The failure modes, in the order they'll hit you

1. **Week 3: the silence.** You've posted 9 times and almost nobody responded.
   This is normal and it is not a signal. The algorithm doesn't know who you are
   yet. Keep going; week 6 is where it starts to move.
2. **Week 5: the drift.** You skip a Monday batch, then a week. Recovery: post
   once, badly, immediately. Don't write an apology post about being away — just
   resume.
3. **Week 8: the comparison.** You'll see someone with 50k followers posting
   worse content than you. They started three years ago. Ignore it.
4. **Week 10: the plateau.** Growth flattens. This is when you look at the
   scorecard and change *one* thing — usually the hook, not the topic.

---

## When to change the plan

Use the scorecard, not your mood.

| Signal | What it means | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Reach is fine, no link clicks | The content works, the ask doesn't | Rewrite the bio and the call to action |
| No reach at all after 6 weeks | Hooks are weak | Rewrite only your first two lines for a month |
| Reach and clicks, no inbound | You're entertaining, not useful | Shift pillars toward the problems you solve |
| Growing but wrong audience | Positioning is off | Back to the worksheet |
