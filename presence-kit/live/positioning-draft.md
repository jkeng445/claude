## Three positioning options

Pick one. They're mutually exclusive in practice — trying to be all three means
being none of them.

### Option A — The applied-AI builder (my recommendation)

> I build AI tools for work where being wrong has a cost — diagnostics, health,
> and frontline operations — and I write about what it actually takes to make
> them safe enough to use.

**Why I'd pick this one:** it's the most defensible. Nearly everyone posting
about AI right now is posting takes. Almost nobody is posting *working systems
with safety constraints they can show*. The LAMP assistant is proof you can
point at, and that's rare enough to be a position.

**Audience:** health-tech founders, NGO and public-health programme leads,
funders, engineers in regulated domains.
**What they should do:** contact you to build or advise.

### Option B — The African-market technologist

> I build technology for African markets — diagnostics, commerce, and culture —
> and I write about what Western-built tools get wrong about this context.

**Why it could work:** strong point of view, underserved audience, and you
clearly have real context. **Why I'd hold it:** it only lands if you have
specific ground-truth stories (a deployment, a field trip, a failed assumption).
If you have those, this beats Option A.

### Option C — The builder-in-public generalist

> I ship one working thing a week — AI tools, brands, systems — and I show the
> whole build.

**Why I'd avoid it as the main line:** it grows fast and converts badly. You get
an audience of other builders, not buyers. Good as a *content format*, bad as a
position. You can absolutely build in public *while* positioned as A.

## If you pick Option A, here's the rest

**One-liner:**
> I build AI tools for high-stakes work — diagnostics, health, frontline
> operations — where a confident wrong answer costs more than no answer.

**Bio line (≤150 chars):**
> Building AI for work where being wrong has a cost. Diagnostics, health,
> frontline tools. I write about what makes them safe enough to actually use.

**The belief that drives your content** (this is your engine — every strong post
comes back to it):
> Most AI products are optimised to look confident. In the places where the
> stakes are real, the valuable behaviour is the opposite: knowing what you
> don't know, and saying so.

That belief is unusual, defensible, provable from your own work, and something a
good number of people in AI would argue with. That's exactly what you want.

## Voice

| You are | You are not |
|---|---|
| Specific, with real numbers and named constraints | Vague, "AI is transforming everything" |
| Willing to say what didn't work | Only showing wins |
| Plain about limits | Hype-y or absolutist |

**No emoji bullets. No "Comment YES below." No rocket ships.** Your whole
position is credibility; engagement bait spends it.
