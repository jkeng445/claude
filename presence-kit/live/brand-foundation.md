## 1. The one fact that should drive your decisions

**Shipping from Nigeria to London or New York costs $40–60 and takes 1–3 weeks.**

On a $60 shirt that's a brand-killer. Your buyer is comparing you to things that
arrive in three days. Most diaspora clothing brands die here, not on marketing.

So the first decision isn't a logo. It's **where the garment is made and shipped
from**, and it has a clean answer: see §5.

## 2. What you're actually selling

Not fabric. The emotional driver for a diaspora buyer is this:

> **Wearing where you're from on an ordinary Tuesday, without it being a
> costume.**

Most people in the diaspora own something traditional. It comes out at weddings,
naming ceremonies, and Christmas. It sits in a wardrobe the other 360 days. The
gap in the market is clothing with real heritage in it that you can wear to
work, to brunch, to the school run.

Everything you post should serve that sentence.

## 3. Positioning

> For **West Africans abroad** who want to wear their heritage without saving it
> for occasions, **[BRAND]** makes **everyday clothing in original Adire indigo
> patterns**, where **every pattern names the technique it comes from and every
> maker is credited** — unlike generic "African print" sold with no source.

**Tagline options** (≤7 words):
- *Indigo, drawn by hand. Worn every day.*
- *Heritage you don't save for Sunday.*
- *Not for occasions. For Tuesdays.*

My pick is the third — it's the sharpest version of the actual insight, and it
will stop a diaspora scroller mid-feed.

## 4. The name

**[CONFIRM] — you decide this one.** My draft is **ARÓ**, the Yoruba word for
indigo dye. It's short, says what the product is, and is in the language of the
craft. Assets in `brand/` use it as a placeholder.

**Before you commit to any name, in this order:**
1. **Trademark search** in class 25 (clothing): USPTO TESS, UK IPO, and Nigeria's
   registry. If a live clothing mark exists, rename now — it's cheap today and
   ruinous later.
2. Domain available (`.com` plus one alternative).
3. Same handle free on Instagram, TikTok, and Pinterest.

Don't print a single label until all three clear.

## 5. Where it's made — pick this before anything else

| Route | Where it ships from | Delivery to buyer | Unit cost (est.) | Honest label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Print-on-demand** (Printful, Printify) | Printed in US/UK/EU | **3–5 days** | Tee ~$14, all-over shirt ~$30–40 | "Adire-pattern print" |
| **B. Artisan-dyed in Abeokuta** | Nigeria, or batch-shipped to you | 1–3 weeks, or local once landed | Varies — get 3 quotes | "Hand-dyed Adire" |

**Recommended: run both, and be loud about the difference.**

- **Route A is your everyday range and your cash flow.** It sidesteps the
  shipping problem entirely, carries no inventory risk, and lets you test which
  patterns people actually buy before you spend anything.
- **Route B is a small capsule — 10–30 pieces — and it is your credibility.**
  Real hand-dyed cloth, real named makers, higher price, limited. It's what makes
  the story true rather than decorative, and it's what press and creators cover.

**The honest-labelling rule, non-negotiable:** a printed piece is an
**"Adire-pattern print."** Only artisan resist-dyed cloth is **"hand-dyed
Adire."** Never blur them. Diaspora buyers know the difference, and heritage
brands that fudge this get publicly dismantled — deservedly.

## 6. Pricing (estimates — verify against live quotes)

| Item | Route | Cost + fees (est.) | Sell | Rough gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tee | A | ~$19 | **$45–55** | ~60% |
| Camp-collar shirt (all-over) | A | ~$42 | **$89–110** | ~55% |
| Scarf | A | ~$22 | **$45–65** | ~60% |
| Hand-dyed capsule shirt | B | varies | **$150–250** | set after quotes |

Below $40 on a tee you're working for free once returns, ads, and failed
deliveries are counted. Diaspora buyers will pay a premium for a real story —
but only if the photos and the site look worth it.

**Payments:** Shopify + Stripe (cards, Apple Pay, PayPal). Not a Nigerian-only
gateway — your buyers are paying in GBP, USD and CAD. Price in the buyer's
currency and show delivery cost before checkout.

## 7. Brand voice

| You are | You are not |
|---|---|
| Specific: "eleko wax-stamp, 80mm repeat" | Vague: "ethnic-inspired vibes" |
| Warm, quietly confident | All-caps, "🔥 DROP 🔥" |
| Crediting the craft and the makers | Implying you invented any of it |
| Plain English, one Yoruba word at a time, explained | Untranslated words used to seem exotic |

## 8. Visual identity

Assets are in `brand/` (placeholder name, swap when yours is confirmed).

| Colour | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Indigo | `#1B2A6B` | Primary — the dye itself |
| Bone | `#F5EFE0` | Undyed cloth; light backgrounds |
| Rust | `#C4622D` | Accent only, ≤10% of any layout |
| Ink | `#0F1838` | Deep text, dark backgrounds |

Indigo on bone measures ~11.5:1 contrast, which passes accessibility standards
comfortably. Rust on bone is ~3.6:1, so use it for large text and shapes only,
never body copy.

**Type:** Fraunces (display) + Inter (body), both free on Google Fonts.

Each product page carries a story card: the technique, what the motif means, and
who made it. That card is the whole differentiator — it's the thing no
drop-shipper can copy.

## 10. Cultural responsibility — write this into the business

1. **Credit the source.** Adire is a Yoruba craft centred historically on
   Abeokuta and Ibadan. Say so on the About page, not in the small print.
2. **Pay makers properly, and say how.** One sentence is enough: "Artisans are
   paid per piece at an agreed rate before anything goes on sale."
3. **Your patterns are original compositions in the Adire tradition.** Never
   copy a living artisan's specific design without a licence and credit.
4. When you get it wrong, fix it publicly and fast.
